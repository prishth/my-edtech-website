MP Patwari Previous Year Question Papers: Download Year Wise & Shift Wise PDFs for Free

The MP Patwari exam is a competitive examination held by the Madhya Pradesh Professional Examination Board (MPPEB) to recruit candidates for the position of Patwari in the revenue department of Madhya Pradesh state.

MP Patwari Previous Year Question Papers are one of the most useful resources for candidates preparing for the Patwari examination. Solving previous papers helps you understand the actual level of the exam, identify repeatedly tested topics, improve time management and become familiar with the type of questions asked in the examination.

On this page, you can find MP Patwari previous year question papers in PDF format, arranged year-wise and, wherever available, shift-wise. Candidates can use these papers as full-length practice tests and analyse their performance before appearing for the upcoming MP Patwari 2026 examination.

MP Patwari Previous Year Paper Pattern

One of the biggest advantages of solving previous papers is that you don't have to rely only on a syllabus list. A syllabus tells you what can be asked. A previous year paper tells you how it is actually asked.

For the current MPESB Group-2 Subgroup-4 examination, the official scheme contains 200 marks divided into two 100-mark sections. The first covers General Science, General Hindi, General English and General Mathematics, while the second covers General Knowledge & Aptitude, General Computer Knowledge, General Logical Reasoning Ability and General Management.

Why MP Patwari Previous Year Papers Matter

PYQs reveal the real difficulty level. Official syllabi list broad topics like General Knowledge or General Mathematics. MP patwari PYQs show you exactly how deep MPESB goes within each topic.

They expose repeat patterns. Certain themes like Madhya Pradesh geography, government schemes, revenue/Panchayati Raj terminology, basic arithmetic, and computer fundamentals show up year after year with only the specifics changed.

They build exam day speed. A CBT with 200 marks in 3 hours (or the earlier 100 marks in 2 hours) rewards candidates who've practiced under real time pressure, not just those who know the syllabus.

PYQs help with sectional prioritization. Once you see how marks are distributed across sections in past papers, you can decide where to invest your remaining prep time for maximum score gain.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1. Are old MP Patwari papers still relevant given the exam pattern has changed?Answer: Yes, largely. The core sections i.e. GK, Maths, Hindi, Reasoning, Computer Knowledge have carried over across every cycle.

Q2. Is there negative marking in MP Patwari? Answer: No. Across previous cycles and the current 2026 rulebook, there is no negative marking for wrong answers.

Q3. How many previous year papers should I solve before the exam? Answer: Aim for at least 8–10 full-length papers/mocks combining genuine previous-year papers and pattern-based mock tests, with a heavier focus on timed full attempts in your final month of preparation.

Q4. Where can I download MP Patwari previous year question papers?

Answer: You can download the available MP Patwari previous year question papers from the paper table on this page. We recommend keeping the papers organised year-wise and shift-wise for easier practice.

Q5. Do previous year questions repeat directly in the new exam? Answer: Direct repeats are rare, especially for current-affairs-based GK questions. However, static topics (Panchayati Raj structure, basic computer concepts, grammar rules, arithmetic formulas) show strong conceptual overlap year after year.

Q6. Is there any difficulty level (LOD) difference across MP Patwari exam shifts, and how is it handled?

Answer: Yes , when an exam is conducted across multiple shifts, some variation in question difficulty between shifts is natural, since each shift gets a different question set. To account for this, MPESB applies a normalization process (the Normalized Equi Percentile method) rather than comparing raw scores directly.
